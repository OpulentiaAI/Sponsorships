# The deck's register — Nocturnal Valley

The campaign half of the voice. The sender's register is the agency's and lives in `references/knowledge/agency/trifecta-profile.md`; this file says what outreach may take from *this* deck and what stays in it.

The decks speak festival-marketing: "immersive" on five of nine slides, "captive, receptive audience," "massive scale," "LET'S BUILD SOMETHING IMMERSIVE." That register is correct **inside the deck** — it is the brochure the prospect reads after replying — and wrong for the email that gets the reply. The banned words are machine-checked from `banned-phrases.json` beside this file.

From the deck, outreach takes **nouns and numbers**: zone names (Astral, Nocturnal, Forest, VIP Experience, Sampling Activations, Creator & Social Media), tier names and ranges, the six pillars, the audience table. It leaves the adjectives.

Two deck arguments are worth carrying in the sender's own words. Early partners get prime positioning and preferred pricing while the festival is new. And the key art's own line: the promoter, Downright Entertainment, is in its tenth year — the festival is a first edition, the operator is not. Both answer the first-edition objection without overclaiming.

Describing the audience: give the recipient the deck's numbers ("24–34, Midwest, $60K–$140K household income") and let them conclude fit. "A captive audience" in an email reads as a threat; the same fact as a number reads as a media kit.
