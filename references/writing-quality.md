# Writing quality

Surface-independent rules for making prose read like a person wrote it. They apply to the invitation, the dossier summaries, and the run report alike. Voice guidance layers on top of them; it does not replace them.

Adapted from the shared `writing-quality` skill in [vercel-labs/marketing-team-eve-template](https://github.com/vercel-labs/marketing-team-eve-template).

## Load this before

- Drafting the invitation
- Writing any prose that reaches a reader — a dossier summary, a reason to engage, the run report
- Reviewing a draft

## Core rules

1. **Kill the AI tells.** Em-dash overuse above all, plus "delve", "leverage", "robust", "comprehensive", "it's not just X, it's Y", and rule-of-three padding. The full list with replacements is in `ai-phrases-to-avoid.md`. The em dash is the strongest single marker: most human writers rarely reach for it, because it is not a key on the keyboard.
2. **Prefer plain English.** Swap bloated wording for the shorter, concrete alternative. `plain-english-alternatives.md` is the lookup table — "use" not "utilise", "about" not "approximately", "because" not "due to the fact that".
3. **Front-load the point.** Lead sentences, paragraphs, and sections with the conclusion. Readers scan, and an invitation gets a second of attention before a decision.
4. **Concrete over abstract.** Show the specific before stating the general, and cut hedges: "just", "simply", "very", "really".
5. **Match the voice you were given.** When working from the community's own material, keep its register. These rules trim noise; they do not impose a personality.

## Why this matters more here than usual

Everything this skill writes is downstream of evidence. A sentence that reads as machine-generated does two kinds of damage at once: it costs the reply, and it undermines the claim it carries. A reader who can tell the message was assembled will discount the dated signal inside it, however well sourced that signal actually is.

The evidence discipline and the writing discipline are the same discipline pointed at different failures.
